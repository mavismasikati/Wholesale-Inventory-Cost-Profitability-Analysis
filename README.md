# Wholesale-Inventory-Cost-Profitability-Analysis
Built an interactive Power BI dashboard to analyse wholesale sales, inventory, costs and profitability. Identified low-margin products, stockouts and supplier delays, providing data-driven recommendations to improve inventory management and increase business profitability.

## Project Overview

This project analyses a simulated Cape Town wholesale business to understand where money is being made and lost.

The business generated **R12.4 million in sales**, but its net profit was only **R156,000 (1.3%)**.

Using Power BI, DAX and Excel, I analysed product profitability, inventory levels, supplier performance and operating costs to identify opportunities to improve business performance.

## Business Problem

The business had strong sales but low profitability. Its sales, purchasing and inventory information was not properly connected, making it difficult to understand:

- Which products generate the most profit?
- Which products have very low profit margins?
- How much money is tied up in inventory?
- Are supplier delays affecting product availability?
- How can the business reduce costs and improve profitability?

## Project Objectives

1. Analyse overall business performance.
2. Identify the most and least profitable products.
3. Monitor inventory levels and stockouts.
4. Evaluate supplier delivery performance.
5. Provide practical recommendations to improve profitability.

### Important Business Insights

**1. High sales do not always mean high profit.**

Cooking oil generates 38% of sales but only 24% of gross profit. Toiletries contribute 31% of gross profit despite representing 21% of sales.

**2. Some products have very low margins.**

Ariel 2kg and Canola Oil 5L generated R623,000 in sales but only approximately R12,000 in gross profit.

**3. Stockouts are affecting potential sales.**

Unmet demand represents approximately R807,000 in lost sales and R165,000 in potential gross profit.

**4. Supplier delays are a concern.**

Only 71% of supplier orders arrived on time. One major supplier achieved an on-time delivery rate of just 64%.

**5. Discounts are reducing revenue.**

The business gave R355,000 in discounts, with 35% of sales order lines receiving discounts of 5% or more.

## Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I identified the following opportunities:

| Recommendation                       |                                       Estimated Impact |
| ------------------------------------ | -----------------------------------------------------: |
| Review prices of low-margin products |                        R40,000 additional gross profit |
| Improve stock availability           |                        R80,000 additional gross profit |
| Reduce slow-moving inventory         |                                  R54,000 cash released |
| Review discount policies             |                           Further savings to be tested |
| Improve supplier performance         | Better stock availability and lower inventory pressure |

**Potential opportunity:** Approximately R120,000 in additional annual gross profit, based on the assumptions used in the analysis.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that the business needs to focus on improving profit margins and product availability rather than simply increasing sales.

By reviewing product prices, improving inventory management and monitoring supplier performance, the business could improve profitability and make better purchasing decisions.

## Limitations

- The dataset is simulated and does not represent an actual company's financial records.
- Financial results are based on the assumptions used in the model.
- Estimated improvements assume sales volumes remain stable.
- Further analysis is needed to confirm the impact of the recommendations.
